5-Point IEP Quick Checklist

A free workflow for special education teachers — complete a standard IEP in under 60 minutes

This checklist follows the actual IEP document structure and can be used with any AI tool (ChatGPT, Claude, or a dedicated SPED AI system). Save this page or print it for your workspace. No signup required.

1 PLOP Analysis — Turn Evaluation Data Into Narrative
This is your foundation. Everything in the IEP flows from a strong Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance (PLOP).
  1. Gather evaluation reports, recent grades, observation notes, and parent concerns
  2. Extract 3-5 specific, data-supported statements about how the disability affects learning
  3. Format as a narrative (not bullet points) in standard IEP language
Example: "The student's reading comprehension, as measured by the WRS-4 (52nd percentile) and classroom assessments (B+ average in structured settings, C- in independent reading), significantly impacts his ability to access grade-level social studies content."
2 Goal Alignment — Every Goal Must Reference the PLOP
Administrators reject IEPs when goals don't connect to PLOP needs. This is the #1 error.
  1. For each area of need in the PLOP, write 1-2 SMART goals
  2. Each goal's baseline must pull directly from the PLOP data
  3. Each goal's target must be measurable (not "improve" — use specific metrics)
  4. If your district requires objectives/benchmarks, break each goal into 3-4 steps
Example: "Given a 3rd-grade passage, [student] will identify the main idea and 3 supporting details in 4 out of 5 trials, as measured by teacher probes, increasing from 1/5 currently to proficiency by the second quarterly review."
3 Service Specificity — Who, What, When, Where
"Services as needed" doesn't pass compliance review. Be specific on all four dimensions.
  1. Who: Name the provider (special ed teacher, SLP, OT, etc.)
  2. What: Specific service (reading intervention, speech therapy, social skills group)
  3. When: Frequency and duration (30 minutes, 3x/week)
  4. Where: Placement setting (general ed classroom, resource room, separate setting)
Correct: "Special education reading instruction, 40 minutes per day, 5 days per week, in a resource room setting with the special education teacher."
4 LRE Justification — Why This Placement?
If the student is in a separate setting for any portion of the day, the IEP must explain WHY the general education setting (with supplements) cannot meet their needs.
  1. State how much time is in general education vs. separate settings
  2. Explain what supplements/benefits were considered for gen-ed placement
  3. Explain why gen-ed alone (even with supports) cannot meet the IEP goals
  4. If the student is fully in gen-ed with pull-out services, explain what happens during pull-out time
Example: "The student will spend 65% of the school day in the general education classroom with related services (speech 3x/week, OT 1x/week). The remaining 35% is in a resource room for reading intervention and math, as these areas require specialized instruction not available in the general education setting with supplementary aids and services."
5 Compliance Cross-Check — Pass Administrator Review
Run through these 4 requirements before submitting. This catches the errors that get sent back most often.
  1. IDEA: Are services directly tied to identified needs in the PLOP? (Not to unrelated issues)
  2. FAPE: Are services specific enough (frequency, duration, location) to provide Free Appropriate Public Education?
  3. LRE: Is the placement justified by PLOP and goals? Does it include rationale for any time outside general ed?
  4. Parent Concerns: Are ALL parent concerns from the eligibility meeting addressed in the document?
Tip: Print the parent concerns from the meeting notes. For each concern, mark where in the IEP it's addressed. If any concern is unaddressed, add it — even with "No further action needed at this time."
