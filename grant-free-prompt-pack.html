🔍 Phase 1: Find & Assess the Right Grant

1. Funder Research Brief
Use when: You've found a potential funder and need to understand if your program fits.

"Act as an expert grant researcher. Analyze the following funder foundation's giving history, priorities, and past awards. Here is the funder's website and recent annual report: [paste URL/text]. Output a brief with: (a) Top 3 giving priorities, (b) Average grant size, (c) Past recipients in education, (d) Key language/themes they use, (e) Fit score for a SPED program serving [your demographic] in [your region] (1-10)."
2. RFP Requirement Extraction
Use when: You have a Request for Proposals and need to parse mandatory vs. nice-to-have criteria.

"Extract every requirement from this grant solicitation [paste RFP text] and organize them into: (a) Mandatory requirements (disqualifying if missing), (b) Evaluation criteria with weights, (c) Required appendices, (d) Page/word limits, (e) Submission format and deadline. Create a compliance checklist I can share with my team."
3. Fit Assessment Matrix
Use when: You're deciding whether to invest time in a specific grant.

"Score our SPED program against this funder's criteria. Our program: [describe — students served, age range, services, geography, budget]. The funder criteria: [paste from RFP]. Output: a scorecard (1-5 per criterion), total score, match percentage, and a recommendation: Pursue, Maybe, or Skip — with reasoning."

✍️ Phase 2: Write the Proposal Fast

4. Concept Note (1-Page Pitch)
Use when: You need a quick proposal preview before writing the full application.

"Write a compelling 1-page concept note for [funder name] about our SPED program serving [demographic] in [location]. Include: problem/need (with local data), our solution (3 core services), expected outcomes (measurable), total budget requested, and why our organization is the right fit. Match the funder's language from their priority areas."
5. Statement of Need
Use when: Writing the needs assessment section (often the most critical part).

"Write a data-driven Statement of Need for a SPED program in [district/region] serving [number] students aged [ages] with [disability types]. Use these local statistics: [paste data]. Include: (a) Current service gaps, (b) Demographic trends, (c) Comparison to state/national averages, (d) Consequences of inaction. Write in the funder's emphasis language: [paste key phrases from RFP]."
6. Program Design & Logic Model
Use when: Defining what you actually do with the grant money.

"Create a complete program design and logic model for a SPED grant-funded initiative. Our program provides: [describe services]. Target population: [describe]. Duration: [length]. Budget: [amount]. Output a structured program design with: (a) Detailed activities (weekly/monthly), (b) Staffing plan, (c) Timeline with milestones, (d) Logic model: Inputs → Activities → Outputs → Short-term Outcomes → Long-term Impact."

✅ Phase 3: Submit Confidently

7. Pre-Submission Compliance Audit
Use when: Final review before hitting submit.

"Act as a strict grant reviewer. Audit this complete proposal [paste proposal] against these requirements [paste RFP requirements]. Flag: (a) Any missing required elements, (b) Contradictions between budget and narrative, (c) Weak or unsupported claims, (d) Areas that don't match funder language. Rate overall compliance: 1-10. List the top 3 fixes needed."
8. Budget Narrative Builder
Use when: Your budget spreadsheet is ready but you need the written justification.

"Write a line-by-line budget narrative for a SPED grant requesting $[amount]. The budget includes: [list personnel, equipment, services, overhead]. Write a clear, persuasive justification for each line item that connects every dollar to student outcomes. The funder prioritizes: [paste criteria]."
9. Evaluation Framework
Use when: Designing how you'll measure success (funders care about this).

"Create an evaluation framework for a SPED program serving [students/services] over [time period]. Include: (a) 5 measurable outcome indicators with baselines and targets, (b) Data collection methods and frequency, (c) Analysis approach, (d) How results will be reported to the funder. Align with the funder's emphasis on [from RFP]."
10. Sustainability Plan
Use when: Proving the program continues after grant funding ends.

"Write a convincing sustainability plan for a SPED program that will end when grant funding stops. Current programs: [describe]. Staff: [describe]. Community partnerships: [describe]. Budget: $[amount]. Projected post-grant: [describe]. Show 3+ concrete strategies for continued funding/operations that don't rely on the original grant. The funder values: [from RFP]."
