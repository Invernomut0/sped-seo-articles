The Problem Every SPED Teacher Knows

The IEP writing process involves:

  • PLOP section (30–45 min): Present levels of performance, combining data from teachers, therapists, and observations
  • Annual goals (30–45 min): Writing measurable, standards-aligned goals with baselines and criteria
  • Services & placement (20–30 min): Determining minutes, frequency, location, and service delivery models
  • Transition planning (15–20 min): Post-secondary goals for students 14+ years old
  • Compliance check (30–60 min): Cross-referencing state and federal requirements, checking dates, signatures, and procedural safeguards
  • Parent communication (15–30 min): Translating technical language into plain English for families

That's 3–4 hours. Some cases take all day. And it's been this way for decades — until now.

Step 1: Intake (15 minutes)

1 Structure your raw data

2 Generate section by section
Use specialized AI prompts — one for each IEP section — that know the specific requirements. A PLOP prompt pulls from your intake data and generates the Present Levels of Performance. What the Tools Actually Look Like

Here's a glimpse of what these AI prompts do — the actual thinking pattern each one follows:

Example: PLOP Section Generator

The prompt takes your structured intake data and produces a Present Levels of Performance section that:

  • Includes academic, social-emotional, and functional domains
  • Cites specific assessment scores and observation data
  • Describes how the disability affects involvement in the general education curriculum
  • Uses the required state-specific language and formatting
  • Flags any missing data fields (so you can follow up before the meeting)

Example: Annual Goals Prompt

This prompt creates 2–4 measurable annual goals, each with:

  • A clear baseline from the PLOP data
  • Observable, measurable criteria (the "how you'll know it's done" metric)
  • A defined timeline and frequency of progress reporting
  • Alignment with state standards (Common Core, state ELA/math frameworks)
  • Accommodations and modifications built into the goal statement
