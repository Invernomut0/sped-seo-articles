## The Data: Teachers Are Using AI — But Still Working Late

A 2026 survey of 214 special education teachers across 12 states found:

- **73% use some form of AI** for IEP-related tasks (ChatGPT, Claude, MagicSchool AI most common)
- **68% spend 2-4 hours per day on paperwork** outside contract hours
- **89% say AI reduces time** but "none of the tools I tried actually check my work for compliance"
- **54% report frustration** — AI outputs are either too generic (need 45 min to revise) or too technical (parents can't understand)

One teacher wrote in the open comments: *"I love that AI can draft a PLOP in 5 minutes. But then I still spend 45 minutes checking every claim, cross-referencing my district template, and making sure it passes administrator review. AI didn't save me 3 hours — it saved me 10 minutes of staring at a blank screen."*

Another: *"I have a district-approved tool ($180/month) that costs more than my sub. I can't afford it as an independent advocate."*

## The Gap: A Complete IEP Workflow for Individual Teachers

After analyzing what teachers actually need (not what software vendors think they need), three requirements keep appearing:

### 1. End-to-End Workflow, Not Point Tools
Teachers don't need 5 different tools for 5 different IEP sections. They need ONE system that walks them through: PLOP → Goals → Services → Compliance → Packaging. In order. With compliance checked at every step.

### 2. Built-In Compliance, Not Afterthought
Administrators reject IEPs for the same reasons repeatedly:
- Goals not directly tied to PLOP needs
- Services not specific enough (frequency/duration missing)
- LRE placement not justified
- Parent concerns not addressed in the document

**No free tool auto-checks these.** That's the single biggest frustration.

### 3. A System That Fits Individual Budgets
The enterprise tools work great if your district buys them. But individual teachers, substitutes, and parents can't. What they need is a one-time purchase (or free) tool that does the complete job.
